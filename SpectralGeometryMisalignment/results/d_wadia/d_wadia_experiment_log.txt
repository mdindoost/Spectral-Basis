SECTION 1: EXPERIMENT OVERVIEW AND MOTIVATION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

--- Central question ---

Rayleigh-Ritz (Y) does two things simultaneously:
  (1) D-orthogonalization: enforces U^T D U = I
  (2) Basis change: rotates features into the Laplacian eigenvector
      coordinate system

The question is: which of these is responsible for the performance gap
between X and Y in softmax regression?

  W (D-ZCA) enforces the same D-orthogonality as Y, but does NOT rotate to
  the Laplacian eigenvector basis. It stays close to the original coordinate
  system (minimum-rotation whitening in the D-inner product sense).

  If W ≈ Y: D-orthogonalization alone explains the entire gap.
  If W > Y (W better than Y): the Laplacian rotation adds extra damage
             beyond what D-orthogonalization alone causes.

  Z (standard Wadia whitening) is included as an additional reference:
  it applies Euclidean whitening (Z^T Z = I) using centered features,
  and measures how much a non-D-orthogonal whitening hurts.


════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 2: VARIABLE DEFINITIONS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

X   Raw node feature matrix of the LCC, shape (n, d). No transformation.
    This is the baseline for all gap computations.

Y   Rayleigh-Ritz restricted eigenvectors, shape (n, d_eff).
    Solves the generalized eigenvalue problem: (Q^T L Q) V = (Q^T D Q) V Lambda
    where Q is the QR decomposition of X (the active subspace basis).
    Enforces U^T D U = I (D-orthonormal).
    Rotates features into Laplacian eigenvector coordinate system.
    span(Y) = span(X) exactly by construction.
    Both D-orthonormal AND Laplacian-rotated.

Z   Standard ZCA whitening of centered X, shape (n, d_eff).
    Enforces Z^T Z = I (standard Euclidean inner product).
    Stays close to original coordinate system (ZCA = minimum-rotation
    Euclidean whitening).
    Computed on centered X_c = X - mean(X, axis=0).
    This is Wadia et al.'s original whitening.

W   D-ZCA whitening of X (Yiannis's formulation), shape (n, d_eff).
    Enforces W^T D W = I (D-weighted inner product, same condition as Y).
    Stays close to original coordinate system — does NOT rotate to
    Laplacian eigenvector basis.
    Computed on raw X (not centered). Uses same D as Y (no self-loops).
    KEY PROPERTY: W and Y have identical orthonormality condition
    (W^T D W = I = Y^T D Y) but different coordinate systems.
    Constructed as: W = X @ V_k @ diag(1/sqrt(eigvals_k))
    where V_k are eigenvectors of C = X^T D X (D-weighted covariance).

D   Degree matrix WITHOUT self-loops.
    D_ii = sum of edge weights incident to node i (binary graph: D_ii = degree_i).
    Used for both Y (Rayleigh-Ritz) and W (D-ZCA). Same D for both.

--- Gap definitions ---

  X_minus_Y = X_acc - Y_acc   (full Rayleigh-Ritz damage)
  X_minus_Z = X_acc - Z_acc   (standard Wadia whitening damage)
  X_minus_W = X_acc - W_acc   (D-orthogonalization damage alone)
  W_minus_Y = W_acc - Y_acc   (KEY: extra damage from Laplacian rotation)

  W_minus_Y > 0: W better than Y → Laplacian rotation adds extra damage
  W_minus_Y ≈ 0: D-orthogonalization alone explains the gap
  W_minus_Y < 0: Y better than W → Laplacian rotation helps (unexpected)

All gaps in percentage points (pp). All accuracies are test accuracy (%).

--- Two modes: why both were run ---

SCALED mode (PRIMARY):
  StandardScaler fit on training nodes only, applied to all four conditions
  (X, Y, Z, W) independently before training.
  Why primary: matches the existing paper pipeline. All MLP experiments in
  the main results used StandardScaler. Scaled results are directly
  comparable to the published Part A gaps.

UNSCALED mode (ROBUSTNESS CHECK):
  No StandardScaler applied to any condition.
  Why run: to test whether W-Y gaps in scaled mode are genuine geometric
  properties or artifacts of StandardScaler interacting differently with W
  vs Y. W stays in the original coordinate system; Y rotates to the
  Laplacian eigenvector basis. Their column distributions differ, so
  StandardScaler applies different effective column scalings even though
  W and Y span the same subspace with the same D-inner product.
  Theoretical prediction: under unscaled SGD, W and Y must perform
  identically. SGD (and all linear methods without coordinate-dependent
  scaling) is invariant to D-orthogonal rotations: if W = Y @ R for some
  D-orthogonal R, the optimal solution quality is unchanged. This means
  unscaled SGD W-Y ≈ 0 is a mathematical prediction, not just an empirical
  hope — it serves as a self-consistency check on the experimental design.

  NOTE on unscaled interpretability:
  In unscaled mode, X has mixed/unknown feature scales, so X_minus_Z and
  X_minus_W comparisons are confounded. Only W_minus_Y is interpretable
  in unscaled mode (W and Y are both unscaled with matching D-inner product).

SECTION 9: INTERPRETATION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

9.1  The theoretical baseline: unscaled SGD
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Unscaled SGD W-Y ≈ 0 on 7/9 datasets is not an empirical approximation —
it is the mathematical prediction confirming itself. W = Y @ R for some
D-orthogonal rotation R (both span the same subspace with the same D-inner
product). Under full-batch SGD without coordinate-dependent scaling, the loss
landscape is identical for W and Y up to the same rotation R applied to the
weight matrix. The optimal test accuracy is therefore the same. The fact that
7 datasets produce exactly 0.00pp and the remaining 2 (cora, pubmed) show
|W-Y| < 1.3pp validates the experiment design: the data pipeline is correct,
D is the same for W and Y, and the experimental apparatus introduces no
systematic bias.

9.2  What scaled SGD tells us
──────────────────────────────
StandardScaler normalizes each feature column by its training-set standard
deviation. W stays in the original coordinate system (close to X's basis);
Y rotates to the Laplacian eigenvector coordinate system. Their columns have
different distributions even though they span the same subspace, so
StandardScaler applies different effective scalings to each. This breaks the
D-orthogonal rotation invariance that makes unscaled SGD W=Y.

The scaled SGD W-Y gaps therefore measure how the Laplacian eigenvector
coordinate system interacts with column-wise normalization — not pure geometry.
The gaps can be large, but require careful interpretation: the +21.48pp citeseer
random SGD is entirely a StandardScaler × training-set-size artifact (see 9.6
Part B) — it vanishes to 0.00pp in unscaled SGD. The genuine large gaps are
on coauthor_cs (+8.24pp fixed, +10.64pp random) and coauthor_physics (+6.18pp
fixed, +6.08pp random), which are consistent across fixed and random splits.

The most stable interpretation of scaled SGD: W consistently outperforms Y on
coauthor_cs (W-Y ∈ [+7.93, +10.64] across all SGD configs) and coauthor_physics
(W-Y ∈ [+6.02, +9.30]), showing that the Laplacian eigenvector coordinate
system is genuinely harder to normalize than D-ZCA's coordinate system on
these two datasets.

9.3  What unscaled Adam tells us
──────────────────────────────────
Adam maintains per-coordinate adaptive learning rates based on the running
second moment of gradients. Even without StandardScaler, W and Y receive
different effective learning rates per column because their column distributions
differ (W: spread across many features; Y: concentrated energy in low-frequency
eigenvectors). This breaks the rotation-invariance that makes unscaled SGD
give W=Y.

The unscaled Adam W-Y gap is a real optimizer-geometry interaction — the
Laplacian eigenvector coordinate system is harder for Adam to navigate than
D-ZCA's coordinate system. This is independent of StandardScaler.

Key numbers from unscaled Adam / lr=0.01 / fixed (the primary comparison):
  coauthor_cs:    W-Y = +9.09pp    (W substantially better)
  amazon_photo:   W-Y = +8.53pp    (W substantially better)
  cora:           W-Y = +2.94pp    (W moderately better)
  pubmed:         W-Y = +3.20pp    (W moderately better)
  citeseer:       W-Y = +0.19pp    (W ≈ Y)
  ogbn_arxiv:     W-Y = +0.00pp    (W = Y)
  wikics:         W-Y = +2.44pp    (W moderately better)
  amazon_computers: W-Y = +0.52pp  (W ≈ Y)
  coauthor_physics: W-Y = +1.97pp  (inconclusive)

At lr=0.1, the unscaled Adam gaps grow further (amazon_photo +19.42pp,
amazon_computers +12.26pp), showing the effect amplifies at larger step sizes
where coordinate-sensitivity matters more.

9.5  Answer to Yiannis's question
────────────────────────────────────
For linear models optimized with SGD without coordinate-dependent scaling
(no StandardScaler, no Adam), D-orthogonalization alone accounts for the
performance gap — W and Y perform identically. The additional rotation to the
Laplacian eigenvector basis adds no damage in this regime. [VERIFIED: CHECK 5]

The Laplacian basis does cause additional damage when optimization is
coordinate-dependent:

  (a) Under StandardScaler (matching the MLP pipeline): W outperforms Y by
      up to +16.50pp on coauthor_cs, +9.30pp on coauthor_physics, +9.73pp
      on cora (scaled, any config). These are the datasets where the
      Laplacian eigenvector columns have different normalization profiles than
      the D-ZCA columns, giving Y a systematic disadvantage under per-column
      normalization.

  (b) Under Adam without StandardScaler: W outperforms Y by up to +9.09pp
      on coauthor_cs, +8.53pp on amazon_photo (unscaled Adam lr=0.01 fixed).
      At higher lr=0.1, the effect amplifies: +19.42pp amazon_photo,
      +12.26pp amazon_computers. This is the optimizer-geometry interaction
      independent of scaling.

The primary mechanism is D-orthogonalization. The basis change is a secondary
effect that amplifies damage through coordinate-dependent optimization.
The magnitude of the secondary effect depends on both the dataset and the
optimizer configuration. It is largest on coauthor_cs and coauthor_physics
(where the effect is consistent across all configurations) and on datasets
with high-dimensional, structured features.

9.6  Citeseer anomalies: Z worse than Y, and random SGD W-Y spike
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

--- Part A: Z worse than Y (fixed splits) ---

Z is consistently worse than Y on citeseer across all configurations:

  Config               Z_acc    Y_acc    X_acc   X-Z (Z worse than X by)
  Adam  lr=0.001 fixed  15.73    19.79    42.23   +26.51pp
  Adam  lr=0.01  fixed  15.40    22.33    43.05   +27.64pp
  Adam  lr=0.1   fixed  15.55    20.28    44.13   +28.59pp
  SGD   lr=0.001 fixed  17.30    22.28    47.10   +29.80pp
  SGD   lr=0.01  fixed  17.45    24.76    48.41   +30.96pp
  SGD   lr=0.1   fixed  25.86    25.86    49.64   +31.47pp

Z is worse than Y (Z_acc < Y_acc) in all 6 configurations. This means
standard ZCA whitening is more damaging than Rayleigh-Ritz on citeseer.

Mechanism: citeseer has 3703 binary features on 2120 nodes, with 1602 linearly
dependent columns (d_eff = 2101 of 3703). The features are extremely sparse.
Z is computed on centered X_c = X - mean(X). For a mostly-zero binary feature,
mean ≈ 0, but centering converts the zeros to small negative values, making
previously-sparse columns artificially dense. ZCA whitening then amplifies
these low-variance artificial non-zeros. The result is a representation that
is more distorted than Y, which uses the Laplacian eigenvector basis without
centering and naturally handles the sparse structure through the graph topology.

This is specific to the centering asymmetry: W (no centering) and Y (no
centering) do not exhibit this pathology — W-Y on citeseer is near 0 in
unscaled Adam (lr=0.001: +0.00, lr=0.01: +0.19, lr=0.1: +1.03) and has the
large scaled SGD gaps driven by StandardScaler interaction (not the ZCA issue).
The Z anomaly is a property of centering + ZCA on sparse binary features,
not a general finding about whitening vs Rayleigh-Ritz.

--- Part B: Citeseer random SGD W-Y spike (+18.99 to +21.48pp) ---

This is a separate anomaly from Part A and must not be conflated with it.

Verified numbers [VERIFIED from scaled/summary.json]:
  SGD lr=0.001 random:  X=71.88  Y=36.45  W=57.50  W-Y=+21.05pp
  SGD lr=0.01  random:  X=72.09  Y=44.23  W=65.71  W-Y=+21.48pp  ← peak
  SGD lr=0.1   random:  X=73.02  Y=47.18  W=66.17  W-Y=+18.99pp

  SGD lr=0.01  fixed:   X=48.41  Y=24.76  W=27.03  W-Y= +2.27pp  ← same lr

Unscaled SGD (all lrs, random): W-Y = 0.00pp [VERIFIED from unscaled/summary.json]

The +21.48pp is the largest W-Y value in the entire experiment. Context:
  - coauthor_cs random SGD (the strongest genuine effect): max +10.64pp
  - citeseer random SGD exceeds it by ~11pp — not a stronger geometric effect,
    but an artifact

Mechanism: The Planetoid fixed split for citeseer uses ~120 training nodes
(~20 per class × 6 classes). The random 60/20/20 split uses ~1272 training
nodes. This 10× increase in training coverage has a dramatic effect on
StandardScaler. With ~120 training nodes, StandardScaler computes noisy
column statistics for both W and Y, and both representations converge poorly.
With ~1272 training nodes, StandardScaler's column-wise normalization is stable.
W (in the original coordinate system, similar to X's basis) benefits strongly
from stable normalization and reaches 65.71%. Y (in the Laplacian eigenvector
basis) has a column distribution where stable normalization provides less
benefit — or may even hurt — reaching only 44.23%. The delta of ~19pp relative
to the fixed split is entirely attributable to how StandardScaler interacts with
each coordinate system at different training set sizes.

This is confirmed by the unscaled collapse: unscaled SGD gives Y=W=25.19% on
the random split (all lrs), proving neither representation is informative
without scaling and the entire gap is induced by StandardScaler.

Conclusion: The citeseer random SGD W-Y spike is a StandardScaler ×
training-set-size interaction, not a property of D-ZCA vs Rayleigh-Ritz
geometry. It should not be reported as a finding about the spectral basis.

    features" is true but is mediated by optimization geometry, not
    information loss.

Q2: The unscaled SGD result (W = Y) is itself a clean, verifiable finding —
    it confirms span equivalence and D-orthogonal rotation invariance of
    linear SGD. Should this be stated as a proposition in the paper?
    It provides a clean theoretical anchor for the experiment design and
    distinguishes our analysis from purely empirical observations.

Q3: Should we run the MLP (3-layer) experiment with D-ZCA features (W) to
    test whether the W-Y gap persists for nonlinear models? A 3-layer MLP
    is not invariant to D-orthogonal rotations (the activation functions
    break the rotation symmetry), so we would expect W > Y even in unscaled
    mode — but the magnitude may differ. This would test whether the basis-
    change effect scales with model nonlinearity.
