SECTION 1: EXPERIMENT OVERVIEW AND MOTIVATION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Rayleigh-Ritz restricted eigenvector transform (Y) changes X in two ways
simultaneously:
  (1) Orthogonal rotation: projects X onto the eigenvectors of the restricted
      Laplacian (Q^T L Q), rotating the feature coordinate system.
  (2) D-orthogonalization: enforces Y^T D Y = I rather than Y^T Y = I.

The D-Wadia experiment (see d_wadia_experiment_log.txt) showed that W (D-ZCA
whitening) achieves the same D-orthonormality as Y without rotating to the
Laplacian eigenvector basis, and W > Y for most datasets. This confirmed that
the Laplacian rotation adds extra damage beyond D-orthogonalization alone.

The present experiment isolates the D-orthogonalization component further by
using standard (Euclidean) eigenvectors (U_std) instead of D-orthonormal
eigenvectors (Y). U_std solves the STANDARD eigenvalue problem rather than
the generalized one:

  Y:     (Q^T L Q) V = (Q^T D Q) V Lambda  →  Y^T D Y = I
  U_std: (Q^T L Q) V =             V Lambda  →  U_std^T U_std = I

Critically:
  - Both Y and U_std use the SAME QR basis Q (same active subspace of X).
  - Both span the SAME subspace (span(Y) = span(U_std) = span(X) exactly).
  - The ONLY difference is the inner product enforced: D-weighted vs Euclidean.
  - No information is lost or gained between Y and U_std.

Primary question:
  Is D-orthonormality (the Y^T D Y = I condition) responsible for the
  performance gap between X and Y?

  If YES: U_std ≈ Y  (Ustd-Y ≈ 0) — removing D-ortho does nothing.
           OR: U_std > Y (Ustd-Y > 0) — D-ortho adds damage.
  If NO:  X ≈ U_std (X-Ustd ≈ 0) — the gap was already present without D-ortho.

Secondary question:
  Does row-normalization (U_rn) of U_std close the remaining gap toward X?
  U_rn[i] = U_std[i] / ||U_std[i]||

Additional motivation:
  This experiment complements the D-Wadia result. W established that Laplacian
  rotation adds extra damage beyond D-orthogonalization. U_std now asks: does
  the D-orthogonalization (the remaining component) contribute to the damage,
  or is the entire gap already present in U_std (the plain eigenvectors)?

SECTION 2: VARIABLE DEFINITIONS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

X      Raw node feature matrix of the LCC, shape (n, d).
       Baseline condition. No transformation.

Y      D-orthonormal restricted eigenvectors, shape (n, d_eff).
       Solves generalized problem: (Q^T L Q) V = (Q^T D Q) V Lambda
       Enforces Y^T D Y = I.
       Rotated to Laplacian eigenvector coordinate system.
       span(Y) = span(X) exactly.

U_std  Standard (Euclidean) restricted eigenvectors, shape (n, d_eff).
       Solves standard problem: (Q^T L Q) V = V Lambda
       Enforces U_std^T U_std = I.
       SAME QR basis Q as Y; same subspace span(X).
       U_std^T D U_std is NOT identity (differs from Y).
       Key isolator: same subspace as Y, different inner product.

U_rn   Row-normalized version of U_std, shape (n, d_eff).
       U_rn[i] = U_std[i] / ||U_std[i]||
       Each row has Euclidean unit norm.
       Tests whether row normalization helps recover accuracy from U_std.

D      Degree matrix WITHOUT self-loops.
       D_ii = sum of edge weights incident to node i (binary graph: degree_i).
       Used for Y (generalized eigenproblem). NOT used for U_std.

L      Unnormalized graph Laplacian, L = D - A (no self-loops).
       SAME L used for both Y and U_std. The only difference is generalized vs.
       standard eigenproblem.

--- Gap definitions ---

  X_minus_Y    = X_acc - Y_acc     (full Rayleigh-Ritz damage)
  X_minus_Ustd = X_acc - Ustd_acc  (damage from eigenvector projection alone,
                                    without D-orthogonalization)
  X_minus_Urn  = X_acc - Urn_acc   (damage from eigenvector projection +
                                    row normalization)
  Ustd_minus_Y = Ustd_acc - Y_acc  (KEY: extra damage from D-orthogonalization)

  Ustd_minus_Y > 0: U_std better than Y → D-ortho adds damage
  Ustd_minus_Y ≈ 0: D-ortho has no effect
  Ustd_minus_Y < 0: Y better than U_std → D-ortho actually helps

  Urn_minus_Y  = Urn_acc - Y_acc   (gap between row-normed U_std and Y)

All gaps in percentage points (pp). All accuracies are test accuracy (%).

SECTION 3: EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Models:
  LINEAR: nn.Linear only (no hidden layers).
          Loss: CrossEntropyLoss, Optimizer: Adam, lr=0.01, weight_decay=0.0.
          Full-batch. 500 epochs. No early stopping.
          best_val_epoch = 500 (fixed) for all seeds.
          Note: weight_decay=0.0 keeps the problem convex. Linear results are
          NOT directly comparable to D-Wadia (different setup: SGD/Adam lr
          sweep, no StandardScaler), but sign of Ustd-Y is still informative.

  MLP: 3-layer (Linear-ReLU-Linear-ReLU-Linear), hidden_dim=256.
       Loss: CrossEntropyLoss, Optimizer: Adam, lr=0.01, weight_decay=5e-4.
       Full-batch. max_epochs=500, patience=100 on val_acc.
       best_val_epoch is the epoch achieving max val_acc across 500 epochs.
       Test accuracy reported at best_val_epoch checkpoint.

Preprocessing:
  StandardScaler fit on training nodes only, applied independently per
  condition (X, Y, U_std, U_rn). Same convention as master_training.py.

Training seeds: 15 (seeds 0–14)
Splits:
  Fixed: 1 split (dataset-specific fixed split, matches paper convention)
  Random: 5 split seeds (60/20/20 stratified, seeds 0–4)

Fixed split summary per entry: 15 seeds
Random split summary per entry: 75 seeds (5 split seeds × 15 train seeds)

Datasets: all 9 (cora, citeseer, pubmed, ogbn_arxiv, wikics,
          amazon_computers, amazon_photo, coauthor_cs, coauthor_physics)
          All using Largest Connected Component (LCC).

--- Relationship to D-Wadia experiment ---

D-Wadia used softmax regression (no hidden layers, no weight decay) with
SGD/Adam at 3 learning rates, unscaled and scaled, weight_decay=0.
This experiment uses Adam lr=0.01 weight_decay=5e-4 with StandardScaler,
matching the paper's main MLP pipeline. Results are not directly comparable
in absolute values, but the direction of gaps (Ustd-Y sign) is the key
comparison.

    Full-rank feature matrices.

  - citeseer: d=3703 but d_eff=2101. 1602 dependent columns. Heavily

  - coauthor_physics: d=8415 but d_eff=7701. 714 dependent columns.

    No parameter count confound between the two conditions.

  All U_std matrices satisfy: max|U_std^T U_std - I| < 1e-6  (Euclidean ortho).

SECTION 7: GAP ANALYSIS — PRIMARY QUESTION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Primary question: Is D-orthonormality responsible for the X-Y gap?

If D-ortho is the cause: X-Ustd should be near zero (the gap vanishes once you
remove D-ortho), or equivalently Ustd-Y should be large and positive.

If the eigenvector projection is the cause: X-Ustd ≈ X-Y (removing D-ortho
barely changes the gap), and Ustd-Y is small.

Key finding: For the 7 datasets where Ustd-Y > 0 (D-ortho harmful):
  fraction_recovered ranges from 2.5% (coauthor_cs) to 20.9% (coauthor_physics).
  The largest absolute Ustd-Y values are wikics(+5.47) and coauthor_physics(+5.26).
  Even in the best case, D-orthonormality accounts for at most 20.9% of the gap.
  The remaining 79–97% of the gap is already present in U_std.

For 2 datasets where Ustd-Y < 0 (D-ortho BENEFICIAL):
  citeseer: D-ortho REDUCES the gap by 2.85pp (13.8% of X-Y).
  pubmed:   D-ortho REDUCES the gap by 4.59pp (23.1% of X-Y).
  In these cases, U_std performs WORSE than Y, meaning the generalized
  eigenvector problem produces better features for the linear classifier.

MAIN CONCLUSION (linear/fixed): [COMPUTED]
  The eigenvector projection onto the Laplacian subspace is the dominant source
  of the X-Y gap. D-orthonormality has a secondary, mixed-sign effect — it adds
  a small amount of damage on some datasets (up to ~21%) and is actually
  beneficial on others (citeseer, pubmed).

--- 7.4  Row normalization (U_rn vs Y) ---

U_rn = row-normalized U_std. Comparison: Urn-Y vs Ustd-Y.

Linear/fixed: [COMPUTED from Section 5.1]
  Urn > Ustd > Y: coauthor_physics (+8.85 vs +5.26), amazon_photo (+6.05 vs +1.29)
    → Row norm amplifies the recovery from D-ortho removal.
  Urn ≈ Ustd > Y: cora, amazon_computers
    → Row norm has little additional effect.
  Y > Ustd ≈ Urn: citeseer (-2.82 vs -2.85), pubmed (-6.05 vs -4.59)
    → Row norm makes things EVEN WORSE than Ustd.
  Ustd > Y > Urn: coauthor_cs (+1.70 vs -0.38)
    → Ustd recovers vs Y but Urn UNDERSHOOTS Y. Row norm harmful for coauthor_cs.
  wikics: Ustd > Urn > Y (+5.47 > +3.51). Row norm partially cancels recovery.

MLP/fixed: [COMPUTED from Section 6.1]
  Urn > Ustd > Y: coauthor_physics (+3.63 vs +1.92)
    → Same as linear: row norm amplifies recovery on coauthor_physics.
  Urn > Ustd, both < Y: amazon_photo (+0.95 vs -0.40), amazon_computers (+0.31 vs -0.38)
    → Row norm on U_std partially recovers but does not match Y.
  Urn < Ustd < Y: cora (-1.60 vs -2.87), pubmed (-5.45 vs -3.39), wikics (-1.15 vs -0.65)
    → Row norm makes already-bad U_std even worse.

Summary: Row normalization of U_std behaves similarly to row normalization of Y
  (the RowNorm model in the main paper). On some datasets it helps, on others it
  hurts. The inconsistency of row normalization is not specific to D-ortho.

SECTION 11: COMPARISON WITH D-WADIA RESULTS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The D-Wadia experiment (linear/softmax, no weight decay) found W > Y on most
datasets (W_minus_Y > 0), meaning the Laplacian rotation adds extra damage
beyond D-orthogonalization. W enforces Y^T D Y = I without rotating coordinates.

This experiment now adds: even if you remove D-orthogonalization entirely
(using U_std which enforces U^T U = I instead), the gap from X remains nearly
as large. U_std is only marginally better than Y (or sometimes worse), while
both are far below X.

--- Hierarchy of damage (from largest to smallest contribution): ---

  (1) Eigenvector projection: projecting X onto the restricted Laplacian
      eigenvector subspace is the PRIMARY source of damage.
      Evidence: X-Ustd ≈ X-Y across most datasets and both models.
      This is the common factor between Y and U_std.

  (2) Laplacian rotation: rotating the coordinate system to align with the
      Laplacian eigenvectors adds SECONDARY damage.
      Evidence from D-Wadia: W (D-ortho, no rotation) > Y (D-ortho + rotation).
      W_minus_Y was positive for most datasets in D-Wadia (Adam lr=0.01).

  (3) D-orthogonalization: enforcing D-weighted inner product adds a TERTIARY,
      mixed-sign effect.
      Evidence from this experiment: Ustd-Y is small and can be positive or
      negative depending on dataset and model.
      For linear model: mostly small positive (D-ortho slightly harmful).
      For MLP: mostly small negative (D-ortho slightly helpful).

Summary: The performance gap is driven by the subspace projection and
eigenvector coordinate system, not primarily by the D-weighting of the
inner product. D-orthonormality is not the culprit.

--- W vs U_std: role definitions ---

W (from D-Wadia):   D-ZCA whitening. Enforces W^T D W = I. Stays in the
                    original coordinate system. Removes D-ortho damage only.
                    Does NOT rotate to Laplacian eigenvector basis.

U_std (this exp.):  Standard restricted eigenvectors. Enforces U^T U = I.
                    Rotates to Laplacian eigenvector basis (solves Q^T L Q V = V Lambda).
                    Does NOT apply D-orthogonalization.

Y:                  Both — Laplacian rotation AND D-orthogonalization.

Therefore:
  W-Y   = benefit of removing Laplacian rotation (rotation damage).
  Ustd-Y = benefit of removing D-orthogonalization (D-ortho damage).
  W-Ustd = difference between the two components.
           Positive: rotation component larger than D-ortho component.
           Negative: D-ortho component larger (or D-ortho helps while rotation hurts).

--- Three dataset groups ---

GROUP 1 — Hierarchy approximately holds (|W-Ustd| < 1pp): [COMPUTED]
  cora (+0.30), ogbn_arxiv (+0.07), wikics (-0.78),
  amazon_computers (-0.74), coauthor_physics (-1.08)
  5 out of 9 datasets. On these, removing the Laplacian rotation and removing
  D-orthogonalization have similar-magnitude effects, and the two can be
  treated as approximately additive components. Note that even here W ≠ Ustd
  exactly; the approximate equality may reflect coincidental cancellations
  rather than genuine independence.

GROUP 2 — Hierarchy fails: rotation >> D-ortho (coauthor_cs): [COMPUTED]
  coauthor_cs: W=41.17, Ustd=26.60, Y=24.89
  W-Y = +16.28pp  (rotation removes 16pp of gap)
  Ustd-Y = +1.70pp (D-ortho removes only 1.7pp of gap)
  W-Ustd = +14.57pp
  Rotation damage is 9.6× larger than D-ortho damage.
  W and U_std are NOT equivalent representations of "intermediate" transforms.
  The additive independence assumption completely breaks down here.
  coauthor_cs is also the dataset with the largest absolute X-Y gap (67.7pp).

GROUP 3 — Components interact, opposite signs (pubmed, citeseer): [COMPUTED]
  pubmed:   W-Y = +2.17 (rotation harmful), Ustd-Y = -4.59 (D-ortho helpful)
            W-Ustd = +6.76
  citeseer: W-Y = +0.14 (rotation negligible), Ustd-Y = -2.85 (D-ortho helpful)
            W-Ustd = +2.98
  On these datasets the two transformations move in OPPOSITE directions relative
  to Y. Removing rotation helps (or is neutral); removing D-ortho hurts.
  This violates the additivity assumption: you cannot treat rotation damage and
  D-ortho damage as independent additive quantities, because the D-ortho
  transformation is beneficial on these datasets (Y > U_std), not harmful.
  The components are not just differently-sized; they have opposite signs.

Additional: amazon_photo (W-Ustd = -2.56, borderline Group 3):
  W-Y = -1.27 (rotation HELPS), Ustd-Y = +1.29 (D-ortho harmful).
  Opposite signs with similar magnitude, but both small. W < Y < Ustd.
  The rotation is constructive here; removing it via W makes things worse.

--- Revised conclusion for Q6 ---

The hierarchy "eigenvector projection → Laplacian rotation → D-ortho" is an
approximation, not a universal decomposition.

It holds approximately on 5/9 datasets (Group 1), where removing the Laplacian
rotation (W) and removing D-orthogonalization (U_std) produce comparably-sized
effects and W ≈ U_std within 1pp.

It fails structurally on coauthor_cs (Group 2): Laplacian rotation accounts
for 16.28pp of the gap while D-ortho accounts for only 1.70pp — a 9.6× ratio.
W and U_std are not interchangeable on this dataset.

It fails directionally on pubmed and citeseer (Group 3): the rotation and
D-ortho components have opposite signs, meaning their effects cannot be summed.
D-ortho is beneficial on these datasets (removing it hurts); the rotation effect
is small or neutral. The damage is NOT from a simple composition of independent
harmful transformations.

Implication for the paper narrative: the hierarchy is useful as a qualitative
organizing principle (rotation > D-ortho for most large-gap datasets), but
coauthor_cs requires the specific statement that the Laplacian rotation
dominates. Pubmed and citeseer require noting that D-orthogonalization is
actually beneficial in the linear setting on these two datasets.

SECTION 12: CONCLUSIONS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

[C1] For linear models, D-orthonormality is NOT the primary cause of the
     X-Y performance gap.
     [COMPUTED] In linear/fixed, X-Ustd accounts for 79–97% of X-Y on the
     7 datasets where Ustd-Y > 0. The dominant factor is the eigenvector
     projection itself, which is shared between Y and U_std.
     This conclusion does NOT hold for MLP: see C3.

[C2] D-orthonormalization has a small, dataset-dependent, sign-variable effect.
     [VERIFIED from Sections 5–7] Ustd-Y ranges from -4.59pp to +5.47pp
     (linear/fixed). The sign flips between datasets and between model types.
     It cannot be characterized as uniformly harmful or uniformly helpful.

[C3] The MLP shows the opposite pattern from the linear model on many datasets.
     [COMPUTED from Section 6] In linear/fixed: 7/9 datasets have Ustd-Y > 0.
     In MLP/fixed: 6/8 datasets have Ustd-Y < 0 (excluding citeseer FLAG).
     The MLP appears to exploit D-orthonormality constructively, possibly because
     D-ortho regularizes the feature distribution in a way that benefits
     nonlinear optimization.

[C4] Row normalization of U_std (U_rn) does not consistently close the gap
     toward X. It helps on coauthor_physics and amazon_photo, hurts on
     pubmed and wikics, and has little effect elsewhere. This mirrors the
     behavior of RowNorm applied to Y in the main paper experiments.

[C5] coauthor_physics is the only dataset with consistently positive Ustd-Y
     across all four model/split combinations, suggesting that on this dataset
     D-orthonormality is a reliable source of additional damage (though still
     small relative to the total gap).

[C6] Extreme underdetermination (r >> 1 in fixed splits for cora, citeseer)
     amplifies the absolute gap sizes but does not change the qualitative
     finding that X-Ustd ≈ X-Y.

[C7] For the paper: the correct narrative is that the performance gap arises from
     projecting features onto the Laplacian eigenvector subspace (the basis
     rotation), not from D-orthonormality. D-Wadia showed that the Laplacian
     rotation is the primary additional factor within the D-ortho family.
     This experiment confirms that removing D-ortho entirely (going to Ustd)
     does not materially close the gap.

Q1. Why does the MLP exploit D-orthonormality constructively (Ustd-Y < 0)
    while the linear model generally does not?
    Hypothesis: D-ortho changes the curvature of the loss landscape in a way
    that helps gradient-based nonlinear optimization even though it does not
    help the optimal linear solution.

Q2. [ANSWERED] Why does coauthor_physics show Ustd-Y > 0 consistently across
    all four conditions, while other datasets are inconsistent?

    Root cause: high homophily + large d_eff + StandardScaler interaction with
    D-orthonormal near-constant columns.

    Evidence (all [VERIFIED] from coauthor_physics/fixed training nodes):

    Step 1 — Column variances before StandardScaler:
      mean_col_var Y_train:     3.63e-6
      mean_col_var U_std_train: 2.90e-5  (U_std has 8x larger mean col variance)

    Step 2 — StandardScaler amplification factor:
      Y columns amplified by 1/sqrt(3.63e-6) ≈ 525x on average
      U_std columns amplified by 1/sqrt(2.90e-5) ≈ 186x on average
      Y is amplified 2.8x more than U_std by StandardScaler.

    Step 3 — Row norms after StandardScaler:
      Y_train:     mean=68.9, std=54.3, min=6.5,  max=572.1
      U_std_train: mean=76.4, std=43.1, min=9.2,  max=227.5
      Row norm range: Y = 88x (572/6.5), U_std = 25x (227/9.2)
      Y has 3.5x more non-uniform row norms than U_std.

    Step 4 — Singular value spectrum after StandardScaler:
      Scaled Y_train top SV: 572.4, sigma_1/sigma_2 = 1.008
      Scaled U_std_train top SV: 251.7, sigma_1/sigma_2 = 1.016
      No dominant first direction in either. Spectral concentration
      does NOT explain the convergence difference.

    Step 5 — Condition numbers:
      kappa_Y = 1.87e9, kappa_Ustd = 1.91e9, ratio = 0.98
      Both equally ill-conditioned. Conditioning does NOT explain
      the convergence difference.

    Mechanism: D-orthonormality enforces Y^T D Y = I using degree weights.
    On coauthor_physics (homophily=0.931, highest of all 9 datasets), the
    leading eigenvectors are nearly constant across nodes — they are very
    smooth. These near-constant eigenvectors have tiny Euclidean column
    variances (~3.63e-6). StandardScaler amplifies them by ~525x, creating
    a small number of nodes with very large feature values (max row norm 572).
    This produces non-uniform gradient magnitudes in Adam's updates.

    U_std enforces U_std^T U_std = I (Euclidean inner product). The same
    eigenvectors are less constrained by node degrees, have larger Euclidean
    column variances (~2.90e-5), and are amplified less by StandardScaler
    (~186x). Row norms are more uniform (max 227). Adam navigates U_std's
    landscape more smoothly.

    Why coauthor_physics specifically:
    The combination of (1) highest homophily of all 9 datasets (0.931) making
    eigenvectors very smooth, (2) large d_eff=7701 giving many such smooth
    eigenvectors, and (3) D-orthonormality which enforces D-weighted rather
    than Euclidean normalization, creates the most extreme StandardScaler
    amplification asymmetry of any dataset. Other high-homophily datasets
    (pubmed: 0.802, cora: 0.804) have either smaller d_eff or fewer smooth
    eigenvectors.

Q3. [ANSWERED] Why does Y have best_val_epoch ~250 on coauthor_physics/MLP
    (7× slower than U_std ~34)?

    Root cause: non-uniform gradient magnitudes from StandardScaler
    amplification of Y's near-constant columns, not ill-conditioning of the
    feature matrix.

    Evidence [VERIFIED from per-seed JSON files, coauthor_physics/fixed]:

    Best_val_epoch across 15 seeds:
      X:     mean=70.3,  std=19.0, min=48,  max=117
      Y:     mean=263.1, std=39.1, min=195, max=310
      U_std: mean=34.5,  std=1.4,  min=33,  max=38
      U_rn:  mean=118.7, std=66.7, min=31,  max=204

    Key observations:
      1. U_std convergence is extremely stable (std=1.4, range=33-38).
         The optimizer finds the optimum in ~34 epochs consistently.
      2. Y convergence is slow AND highly variable (std=39.1, range=195-310).
         Different seeds take 195 to 310 epochs.
      3. Y val_acc oscillates rather than converging monotonically.
         Example seed 0: 61.8% → 73.4% → 71.4% → 79.8% → 87.7% → 72.5% → 85.2%
         U_std seed 0: smooth rise to 87.9% then stable stop at epoch 36.

    What conditioning shows (ruling out the obvious hypothesis):
      kappa_Y = 1.87e9 ≈ kappa_Ustd = 1.91e9 (ratio=0.98)
      Both are equally ill-conditioned. Conditioning does NOT cause the 7x gap.
      sigma_1/sigma_2: Y=1.008, U_std=1.016 — no dominant direction in either.
      Spectral concentration does NOT cause the gap.

    Actual mechanism — row norm heterogeneity after StandardScaler:
      Y_train row norms:     mean=68.9, std=54.3, min=6.5, max=572
      U_std_train row norms: mean=76.4, std=43.1, min=9.2, max=227

      Y has nodes with row norm 572 and nodes with row norm 6.5 — an 88x
      range. These extremes create gradient magnitude spikes during Adam
      updates: nodes with large row norms produce large gradients that push
      parameters into poor regions, then the optimizer corrects, overshoots,
      and oscillates. U_std's row norm range is 25x (227/9.2) — 3.5x more
      uniform. Adam converges without oscillation.

      The oscillatory val_acc trajectory of Y (visible in seed 0, 1, 2 curves)
      is the direct consequence of this gradient non-uniformity, not of local
      minima, saddle points, or numerical instability.

    U_rn (row-normalized U_std) is bimodal:
      Some seeds converge at ~31-34 epochs (like U_std), others at ~100-204
      epochs (like Y). mean=118.7, std=66.7 — high variance reflects two
      regimes. Row normalization creates new non-uniformity in the gradient
      landscape that is seed-dependent.

    Summary: The 7x convergence difference between Y and U_std on
    coauthor_physics is caused by StandardScaler amplifying Y's near-constant
    D-orthonormal columns by ~525x (vs ~186x for U_std), producing outlier row
    norms that destabilize Adam's gradient updates. This is a StandardScaler-
    D-orthonormality interaction specific to high-homophily graphs with large
    d_eff.

Q4. Why does citeseer/fixed/MLP collapse completely for Y and U_std (both)
    but not for X? Both Y and U_std have extreme underdetermination (r=157.6),
    but X also uses d_X=3703 features (not much different from d_eff=2101).
    The collapse affects ALL spectral conditions, suggesting the issue is with
    the QR-projected subspace, not specifically D-ortho.

Q5. Does the Ustd-Y effect persist if weight decay is removed (wd=0) as in
    the D-Wadia experiment? The D-Wadia results used wd=0; this experiment
    uses wd=5e-4. Regularization might interact with the D-ortho structure.

Q6. [ANSWERED — see Section 11b] Is the "secondary damage from Laplacian
    rotation" (found in D-Wadia as W_minus_Y > 0) consistent with the
    hierarchy established here? If W > Y (rotation harmful) and Ustd ≈ Y
    (D-ortho negligible), then W ≈ Ustd would be expected. Checking W vs
    Ustd directly closes the loop.
    W vs U_std comparison reveals three groups:
    - Group 1 (5/9 datasets): |W-Ustd| < 1pp — hierarchy approximately holds.
    - Group 2 (coauthor_cs): W-Ustd = +14.57pp — rotation 9.6× larger than D-ortho.
    - Group 3 (pubmed, citeseer): W and Ustd move in opposite directions vs Y —
      components are not additive; D-ortho is beneficial on these datasets.
    The hierarchy is a useful approximation but not a universal decomposition.
